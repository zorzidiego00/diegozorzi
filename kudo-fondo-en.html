The problem

On long rides the helmet becomes a nuisance

Riders with a long or round head live with pressure points, and heavy sweaters end up with wet glasses. Current helmets only adjust the retention system, not the surface that rests on the head.

The problem

Fit and sweat

Pressure points after hours in the saddle, sweat that soaks the padding and drips onto the glasses.

The solution

Interchangeable pads

Soft pads in three zones and several thicknesses, combined into four recipes for four head shapes.

The strategy

A platform

Launch on gravel, then extend to road and mountain bike.

01 · Market and benchmark

No gravel helmet adapts the surface that rests on the head

Gravel is the growing segment of cycling, and no gravel helmet works on the contact surface: that is the space Kudo Fondo takes.

Trends

  • Gravel is growing. In Austria it is the fastest-growing category: 29,000 bikes sold, +48.3%, +74% for non-electric models [1]. Paid market reports give estimates that contradict each other, so I do not use them.
  • The gravel helmet is a young category. Until recently, helmets had stayed out of gravel specialisation [2]. Now launches are multiplying.
  • Features are converging. Rotational protection, eyewear docking and a light mount are now found even at €90: they no longer differentiate.

Positioning

List prices from the benchmark sources; POC at the midpoint of €200-220; KAV Portola, a made-to-measure 3D-printed helmet, as a reference [4]. The customisation scale is my own, based on manufacturers' descriptions: from level 0, circumference only, to level 4, a fully made-to-measure helmet.

Kudo Fondo moves one level above the Kudo at the same price, and gets close to made to measure without the waiting time.

The first market: a beachhead approach

I do not launch the platform across the whole range at once. I follow Geoffrey Moore's beachhead approach (Crossing the Chasm, 1991), later formalised by Bill Aulet at MIT: win a narrow segment and use it as a base to expand into neighbouring segments, one after another [5].

First market

Long-distance gravel

Where fit and sweat matter most.

Second market

Road

The head-shape benefit is already supported by evidence.

Third market

Mountain bike

The problem is stability: the benefit must first be proven with dedicated tests.

The concept was born for startups and new technologies. I use an adapted form: for an established brand it reduces the launch risk of a new platform.

02 · User and need

Three personas, one for each market

Luca defines the first market; Giulia and Andrea justify the extension to road and mountain bike. The personas are archetypes: names and ages are illustrative, while problems and behaviours come from forums, reviews and technical sources.

Gravel · first market

Luca, 38

Rides 4-6 hours on gravel at weekends and always wears sunglasses. He wants to finish the ride without the helmet becoming a nuisance.

  • Heat on slow climbs: uphill the speed drops and the wind no longer cools [6].
  • Sweat that soaks the padding: once saturated, it lets a steady trickle run down [7].
  • Short-lived fixes: sweatbands last about half an hour of hard effort [8].

He would value the anti-sweat front pad and the spare parts.

Road · second market

Giulia, 31

Rides on the road 3-4 times a week, has a long oval head and has changed three helmets in four years. Her problem is pressure that builds up after hours.

  • Long heads: even the largest size squeezes at the front and at one spot on the back of the head [9].
  • Round heads: pressure on the temples with some brands [10].
  • Even a premium helmet may not fit: the Kudo reviewer marked it down partly because of the fit on his head [11].

She would value a helmet better suited to the particular shape of her head, and being able to fine-tune its fit.

MTB · third market

Andrea, 44

Trail and light enduro at weekends. His helmet moves with the vibrations, as it does not sit fully in contact with his head, and to keep it still he has to tighten it too much.

  • Vibrations make it worse: bumps and jolts increase helmet movement [12].
  • Stability is an MTB requirement: the ideal helmet stays put on rough terrain [13].
  • Tightening creates pressure: loose, the helmet wobbles; tight, it presses on the forehead [9].

Hypothesis Whether the pads improve stability must be tested: that is why MTB is the last market.

As I could not run interviews, in a real project these hypotheses would be validated with fit tests on a sample of riders.

03 · The concept

Comfortable from the first hour to the last

Kudo Fondo adapts the helmet to the shape of the head, keeps the forehead dry and is renewed by replacing only the worn parts, through a system of interchangeable pads.

Why a pad system

In the benchmark, every helmet adjusts fit only through the retention system, a ring that tightens around the head at one height. None works on the surface that rests on the head, where pressure points and gaps arise. That is exactly where the pads work.

The idea has a precedent in motorcycle helmets: Arai offers different internal shapes and removable padding to fit the helmet to the head [14]. In bike helmets this approach does not exist yet.

How it works

  • Three zones: forehead, temples and back of the head. In each zone a soft pad attaches with hook-and-loop, available in several thicknesses.
  • Four recipes: long oval, intermediate oval, round and very round. The number comes from studies on head shapes: with three, very round heads would be left out; with five, the extra variants would differ by only a few millimetres.
  • Anti-sweat front pad: it includes a soft barrier that carries sweat past the edge of the glasses. It is the zone that wears most.
  • Spares, not waste: each pad is replaced individually, so the helmet lasts longer.

The four recipes follow the most common head shapes in anthropometric studies. Pad thickness is drawn in proportion to the space between head and helmet, exaggerated for legibility; in a real project thicknesses and combinations would be set through fit tests.

How the recipe is chosen

Online

A photo from above

With a tight cap and a credit-card-sized card for scale, plus the circumference measured with a tape. The circumference sets the size, the photo sets the recipe.

In store

The fitting kit

The retailer has every pad and finds the combination together with the customer.

After purchase

One pad at a time

If a pressure point appears, the customer buys only the pad for that zone.

Since a correction has a cost, the app shows how reliable its suggestion is and, when reliability is low, recommends a fitting in store before buying.

A platform that replaces the Kudo

The pad system can work across the whole fizik range. Kudo Fondo starts on gravel at the same price as the Kudo. At launch both helmets stay in the catalogue; once validated on the market, Kudo Fondo takes the Kudo's place and then extends to road and mountain bike.

Risks

  • Rotational protection: on the Kudo the MIPS Air Node system is built into the padding [15]. Pads of variable thickness must be developed with the system's supplier.
  • Certification: soft pads do not absorb impact. A maximum thickness is needed, with tests on the least favourable configuration.
  • Photo accuracy: a wrong suggestion costs the customer an extra pad and spoils the experience.
  • Intellectual property: there are patents on padding that diverts sweat [16]. A freedom-to-operate check is needed.

04 · Product brief

Same price as the Kudo, better fit, revenue from spares

Kudo Fondo costs the same as the Kudo, €290, and replaces it: the customer gets a personalised fit and sweat management at the same price, and fizik adds revenue from spare parts. The working hypothesis, to be checked against company data, is that the pads add little to the helmet's component cost.

Objective and positioning

Bring into the Kudo range a helmet that solves the two most common problems on long rides, fit and sweat on the glasses, starting from gravel.

  • Customer: gravel riders who ride for several hours, with attention to those who struggle to find a helmet of the right shape.
  • Price: €290, like the Kudo. It is not a cheaper version but its evolution. At launch the Kudo and Kudo Fondo both stay in the catalogue; once validated on the market, Kudo Fondo replaces the Kudo.
  • Channels: specialist shops with a fitting kit, fizik e-commerce with recipe suggestion from a photo.

Requirements

Must have

  • Pads in three zones and several thicknesses, attached with hook-and-loop.
  • Four reference recipes for head shapes.
  • Anti-sweat front pad, effective even with the head tilted forward in riding position.
  • Pads replaceable individually, sold as spares.
  • Pad seats not interchangeable between zones, to avoid fitting mistakes.
  • Rotational protection compatible with the pads.
  • EN 1078 certification for Europe and CPSC 1203 for the United States.
  • Three sizes like the Kudo: S 52-56, M 56-59, L 59-61 cm.

Should have

  • Online recipe suggestion, with a reliability indicator.
  • Fitting kit for shops.
  • Ventilation at least equal to the Kudo, weight in line with the Kudo.
  • Eyewear docking, fizik rear light included.

Nice to have

  • Design coordinated with the Terra line.
  • Provision for a crash detection module.

Out of scope

  • Aerodynamic optimisation, already covered by the Kudo Aero.
  • Integrated electronics.

Regulatory constraints

The European standard EN 1078 sets the impact absorption tests and the retention system requirements: the pads must pass them in every thickness configuration [17]. According to public sources the 2012 version is still the reference. A revision is under way (draft prEN 1078, 2023) that adds criteria on rotational impact absorption [18]: a relevant point for compatibility between the pads and rotational protection. In a real project the applicable version would be confirmed with the certification lab.

Pad pricing

Replacement pads on the market range from €3-16 for simple ones to about €28-40 for kits with integrated MIPS Air Node [19]. Giro's kit with sweat management costs $18 [20].

05 · Communication and launch

Show before you claim

The launch aims to get people to try the pad system: it is the new part, and it has to be shown before it is claimed.

The name

I stay in the Kudo family because the new helmet is its evolution. "Fondo" recalls comfort on long rides (in Italian, "di fondo" means endurance riding).

Positioning

For gravel riders who spend many hours in the saddle, Kudo Fondo is the fizik helmet that adapts to the shape of the head and keeps the forehead dry, through a system of interchangeable pads. Unlike other helmets, which only adjust the retention system, it works on the surface that rests on the head.

Key messages

01

Fits like your head.

Four pad recipes for four head shapes, chosen from a photo or in store.

02

Dry forehead, clean glasses.

The front pad carries sweat away from the glasses.

03

Renew it, don't bin it.

When a pad wears out, you replace only that one.

Each message is used only once the brief's tests have confirmed it, especially the second. An unproven promise, in a community that talks a lot, is the fastest way to lose credibility.

Launch plan

Before

Tests and training

Field tests with riders of the four head shapes, whose feedback also becomes content. Training selected shops on the fitting kit. App trial with a small group of users.

Launch

Before the season

Presentation a few months before a major gravel event. Pad kit trials at events and partner shops, content showing how the recipe is chosen.

After

Data and spares

App data to refine the recipes, customer feedback to time the extension to road, spares as a product in their own right.

The sales network: the helmet in 60 seconds

Opening

"Does your helmet give you pressure points, or wet your glasses with sweat on long rides?"

Product

"Kudo Fondo adapts to the shape of your head with inner pads, and the one on the forehead diverts sweat."

Trial

"Let's try it with the kit: in two minutes we'll find your combination."

Close

"When a pad wears out, you replace just that one, not the helmet."

The trial is the decisive moment: that is when the customer feels the difference from any other helmet on the shelf.

How I would measure success

  • Fit: fit-related returns and exchanges, compared with the current Kudo.
  • System adoption: share of customers who use the app or fitting kit before buying.
  • Spares: pad sales after the first year, a sign the system is really used.
  • Satisfaction: reviews mentioning fit and sweat.
